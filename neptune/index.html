The idea

One loop, three jobs for AI

Pick a stage. Each one feeds the next.

Fishing is thecommon language

AI helps design and build the product

AI agents scope, write and review the app under a written rulebook. Corey approves the scope and every merge, then tests it where it counts: he fishes with it on every trip.

  • 4,702 commits and 1,212 merged changes since February 10, 2026
  • 34 automated workflows keep data fresh and checks running
  • 214 changes an angler can see, each logged in plain words

AI helps promote it, one water and one business at a time

A curator agent drafts the weekly Friday Read for each water from that week's data. Each guide or outfitter is researched and gets a listing built before the first conversation, so the pitch is "see yours, switch it on."

  • 64 Friday Read editions published, one per water per week
  • 52 local businesses researched and given a pre-built listing; 6 live
  • 2,954 listing views and 460 opens tracked for partners

Then the app teaches AI to people who would skip a tech talk

An angler who sees how the call for their home lake was made understands what AI can do and where it needs checking. The same build is taught through workshops, a podcast and a nine-part series on the signals behind the score.

  • Featured among local AI builds at a UA Little Rock-hosted meetup of 30+ people, September 2026
  • Prompt Play workshops and the Tale Waters podcast, both launched in 2026
  • Sources shown and estimates labeled on everything AI touches

Where it is headed

A ground-level R3 platform

Recruit, retain, reactivate. Rated honestly.

A record 57.9 million Americans fished in 2024, and 16.6 million left the sport that same year. Keeping anglers is the industry's own marketing problem. Here is how far one app in one state has got.

The builder

In 2019 it took a team. In 2026, AI is the team.

Before Pocket Fishing Guide, founder Corey Boelkens ran RaftUp Technologies, which partnered with the Miami International Boat Show on its water taxi app, onsite arrival screens and a staffed control center.

Rides rose 18 percent to nearly 60,000. The same idea, live data turned into a clear decision, is what he rebuilt for anglers in 2026. He still tests it the same way: on the water, every trip, and now for planning photo and film shoots around dam releases.

Sources: Boating Industry, November 2018 · Marina Dock Age, March 2019

What this does not prove yet

  • Reach is early. 723 people used the app in its first 90 days, by its own analytics. Sign-in opened June 7, and 29 anglers have confirmed accounts.
  • There is no license measure. Nothing here shows that a license was bought or renewed because of the app.
  • Interest is not a partnership. The agency conversations are real and unsigned.
  • The numbers are first-party, read on November 24, 2026, and not audited by anyone else.

The claim is narrower and, we think, more useful: a marine business of any size can run this playbook. The rulebook, the agents and the human gates are the part that carries over. More in the case study.
