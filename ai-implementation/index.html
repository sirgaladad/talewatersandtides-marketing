Week by week

The 90 days, in five stages.

We pick two workflows that eat your week, usually inquiries and follow-up, and make AI handle the first draft of each. Nothing reaches a customer without a human yes.

  1. Weeks 1–2Assess

    Find the two workflows

    • Half-day on site watching how work moves
    • Inventory of tools you already pay for
    • Baseline: hours, response time, misses

    You get: the one-page plan

  2. Weeks 3–4Implement

    Build it in your tools

    • Draft-and-approve set up in booking, email or CRM
    • A person named as owner for each workflow
    • Test mode first

    You get: both workflows live, owner trained to approve

  3. Weeks 5–6Train

    Train on real work

    • Two 90-minute sessions with your team
    • Cheat sheet per role
    • Switch from test to live

    You get: the team running it

  4. Weeks 7–10Run

    Run it with you

    • Weekly review of what AI drafted and what got changed
    • Fix the prompts, not the people
    • Guardrails where it drifted

    You get: the runbook

  5. Weeks 11–13Improve

    Read the numbers

    • Hours saved, response time, misses against the baseline
    • Decide: stop, keep, or add a third workflow
    • Hand-off or monthly tune-up

    You get: day-90 numbers and a recommendation

Questions

Straight answers.

What does it cost?

One quote for the whole 90 days, written after the first 30-minute call once we know the two workflows and the tools involved. No hourly billing and no change mid-engagement. Ask on the call and you will get a number, not a range.

Who is this for, and who is it not for?

Owners of service businesses with roughly 5 to 50 people: clinics, salons and studios, trades and field service, professional services, retail and hospitality. It is not for a team that wants a chatbot on the website and nothing else, or one where nobody can own a workflow after I leave.

Do we need to buy new software?

Usually not. The work happens inside the email, booking, CRM and office tools you already pay for. If something is missing we find out in weeks 1 to 2 and decide together before anything is bought.

How do you measure whether it worked?

Weeks 1 to 2 set the baseline: hours spent on the two workflows, response time to inquiries, and misses. Day 90 reports the same three numbers, in plain language, with a recommendation to stop, keep, or add a third workflow.

What if my team does not use it?

Training is on their own inbox and their own customers, not slides, and a named person owns each workflow from week 3. Weeks 7 to 10 are spent fixing prompts, not people. If a workflow still is not used, we say so at day 90 and it does not go in the runbook.

What happens after day 90?

You get the runbook and keep every account. Most owners choose a monthly tune-up; some run it themselves. Either way there is no lock-in: leave when it works.

Tell me what's not working.

Thirty minutes tells us whether it is an afternoon fix or a 90-day engagement. Either answer is useful.
