# Tale Waters & Tides Guiding — Offer, Pricing & Website Plan

Oct 9, 2026 · @Corey Boelkens

## TL;DR

Launch as a White River–only trout service, with the lower White at the Buffalo confluence as your signature water. No park permit is needed as long as you and your clients stay on the White and off National Park land. Price near Gaston's, not under it, and win on what's included and on the story you send home.

- **The boundary rule:** Buffalo National River runs all the way to the mouth. Guiding is fine on the White itself, but don't run clients up into the Buffalo, and don't beach or hold shore lunch on Buffalo gravel bars. Confirm exactly where the line sits at the mouth with the park's Commercial Services office (870-365-2724).
- **Benchmark:** Gaston's charges $430 half day / $550 full day for 1–2 anglers in 2026, plus a $30 bait fee and $50 per person for shore lunch. Guide cancellations there are non-refundable.
- **Launch lineup:** three White River packages — Half Day ($400), Full Day with shore lunch ($550), and the signature Confluence Day ($600). Details in Packages. Sold first to Central Arkansas, with Little Rock departures as the exclusive hook.
- **Website:** one new `/guiding` section on talewatersandtides.com with a single booking path. A visitor should go from "which trip?" to a paid deposit in under 3 minutes on a phone.
- **Differentiator:** the "Tale" in your brand. Every trip ends with a written-and-photo trip story delivered within 48 hours. Gaston's sells heritage; you sell the story.

## Interlocks that gate launch

Nothing goes on the website as bookable until rows 1–4 are green. With the Buffalo off the plan, the National Park permit drops out and becomes one boundary rule (row 5).

| # | Requirement | Applies to | Cost / timing | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | AGFC Resident Fishing Guide License (GLF) — required to guide, aid or assist anyone fishing for pay | All trips | $25/yr; does not include your own fishing license | Open |
| 2 | Your own AR fishing license + trout permit | All trips | \~$20/yr | Open |
| 3 | USCG captain's license (OUPV / "six-pack") — many Ozark guides hold one for paid passengers on navigable water | Motorized White River trips | \~2–4 months for course + medical + drug test | **Verify** with USCG whether your stretch counts as navigable |
| 4 | Commercial general liability + watercraft insurance naming the business | All trips | Quote needed | Open |
| 5 | Stay off Buffalo National River with clients: no running up the Buffalo, no landings or shore lunch on park gravel bars or banks | Confluence Day | Free; one call to NPS Commercial Services (870-365-2724) to confirm the line at the mouth | Open |

How the boundary shapes the product at the confluence:

- Launch and take out at White River access points or a partner resort's ramp, not at park landings on the Buffalo.
- Pick shore-lunch spots on the White's own banks with landowner permission (a partner resort's frontage is ideal).
- Market the confluence for its scenery and water. Never advertise fishing or guiding "on the Buffalo".
- If you ever want Buffalo trips back, the NPS window reopens in a later application cycle; nothing here blocks that.

Also worth adding before launch: CPR/First Aid and a written emergency plan (NPS asks for a safety plan anyway), a signed liability waiver per client, and a simple LLC or DBA so the guide business sits apart from TWT consulting.

What the research says about positioning:

- **Gaston's wins on heritage and lodging**, not on the guide experience itself. Its booking is phone-only and its add-ons are à la carte. A guide who includes lunch, tackle and a clear online booking flow beats it on convenience at the same headline price.
- **The confluence is under-served.** One resort covers Buffalo City; most White River guides launch well upstream near Bull Shoals, Cotter and Norfork. A lower-White day built around the confluence has almost no direct competition.
- **Smallmouth guiding on the Buffalo is thin** because of the NPS permit, the 10 hp limit and the alternating-year application window. Without the permit, sell the confluence for its scenery and water, never as Buffalo River fishing.
- **Gaston's guides average 20–40 years on the water.** You won't out-tenure them at launch; compete on teaching, storytelling and service instead.

## Packages and pricing

Three White River packages, all live at launch. Prices are for 1–2 anglers, with a third angler at +$100; each includes boat, fuel, rods, tackle and bait, so there are no surprise line items.

| Package | Length | Price (1–2) | Includes | Who it's for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| White River Half Day | 4 h, morning or afternoon | $400 | Boat, tackle, bait, drinks and snacks, photo recap | First-timers, families, travelers on a tight day |
| White River Full Day | 8 h | $550 | All of the above + guide-cooked shore lunch + written trip story within 48 h | Couples, serious anglers, gift buyers |
| Confluence Day (signature) | 9 h | $600 | Full Day + the lower White down to where the Buffalo comes in, lunch on the White's bank, trip story | Anglers who want the "only here" day, destination travelers |

Why these numbers:

- **Half day at $400** sits $30 under Gaston's while including bait ($30 there). Real saving to the client is \~$60; you still price at the market midpoint.
- **Full day at $550** matches Gaston's headline but includes the shore lunch Gaston's bills at $50 a person. For two anglers that is a $100 better value with zero price objection.
- **Confluence Day at $600** is a modest premium for the longer run, extra fuel and a stretch few guides work. Lower than the $675 first proposed, because it's now one river.
- **Launch offer, not launch discount:** the first 20 trips get a free printed trip story or photo book instead of a lower price. Price anchors are hard to raise later; extras are easy to retire.

Add-ons and policies to publish with the packages:

- No fly fishing: every trip is spinning gear and bait, with all rods and lures provided. Say so in the FAQ.
- Gift cards in any package amount (Gaston's sells these; they are an easy holiday revenue line).
- Deposit: 50% at booking. Full refund to 14 days out; within 14 days, deposit converts to a credit good for 12 months.
- Weather and water: if the guide cancels for lightning, high or muddy water or unsafe generation, the client chooses a full refund or a new date. This beats Gaston's non-refundable rule and is a selling point.
- Not included and said plainly: Arkansas fishing license + trout permit, lodging, and gratuity (suggest 15–20%).

Deferred ideas, worth testing after 30 trips: a 2-day Confluence weekend with a partner cabin, a corporate "leadership on the river" day tying into TWT consulting, and a women's or kids' intro clinic.

## Little Rock departures and the home market

You pay the Little Rock–to-river drive on every trip, not just the shuttle ones, so price it in two ways: a Little Rock Departure product that charges for the ride, and "river run" dates that spread the drive across several trips.

The drive, per trip (verify on your own route and vehicle):

| Item | Estimate | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Distance, Little Rock to Cotter / Buffalo City area | \~150 mi each way, \~300 mi round trip | Approximate; check your exact launch |
| Drive time | \~3 h each way | No trailer on the highway; hitch up in Mountain Home |
| Fuel, no towing (boat stored in Mountain Home) | \~$35–$40 | \~25 mpg, \~$3/gal |
| Full vehicle cost | \~$200–$225 | \~70¢/mile all-in |
| Your time | \~6 h unpaid | Before any fishing |

Storing the boat in Mountain Home removes the long tow, but the \~6 h round trip remains. On a $550 full day with no travel charge, the drive still eats roughly 35–40% of revenue before boat fuel, bait, lunch and insurance. That's the case for the two models below.

**Model 1 — Little Rock Departure (the exclusive).** Clients meet you at a fixed Little Rock pickup point and ride up with you.

| Package | Day shape | Price (1–2) | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Little Rock Departure — Half Day | Leave 5:30 a.m., \~5 h fishing, home by \~6 p.m. | $650 | The default departure trip; a full day plus 6 h of driving makes a 15-hour day you shouldn't drive tired |
| Little Rock Overnight — Confluence Weekend | Drive up the evening before, Confluence Day, home that evening | $1,100 + lodging at cost | Partner lodge rate; best for couples and small corporate groups |
| Third guest | — | +$150 | Covers a seat and lunch |

**Model 2 — River runs.** Publish meet-at-the-river dates only in 2–3 day blocks. Drive up once, guide 2–4 trips, stay at the family house in Mountain Home where the boat is stored, so lodging costs nothing. The \~$200 drive cost then drops to $50–$100 per trip at the published $400 / $550 / $600 prices.

New interlock before anyone rides with you: a personal auto policy usually excludes carrying passengers for pay. Get commercial auto (or a hired/non-owned rider on the business liability policy) and confirm whether Arkansas treats paid passenger transport as for-hire carriage. Until that's settled, offer "caravan from Little Rock" (clients follow in their own car) at the river price. Also tell your boat insurer where the boat is stored and that it's used commercially, and meet clients at the ramp rather than the family house.

Central Arkansas first, then branch:

1. **Little Rock / North Little Rock / Conway (launch market).** Position as "Little Rock's White River guide — we pick you up." Channels: your own networks and events, local fly and outdoor clubs, Little Rock employers for team days, and a `/guiding/from-little-rock` page targeting searches like "trout fishing trips from Little Rock."
2. **Holiday gift cards now.** It's October; trips open in March. Sell Little Rock Departure gift cards for the holidays so the first season is partly pre-booked and pre-paid.
3. **North Central Arkansas (after 20–30 trips).** Mountain Home, Harrison and partner-lodge guests, who meet you at the river on river-run dates.
4. **Branch out (year two).** Hot Springs, Northwest Arkansas, then Memphis and Dallas destination anglers through the Confluence Weekend.

## Booking stack and policies

Use a flat-fee booking tool on your own site and treat marketplaces as paid lead sources, not your front door. At one boat and a few trips a week, a percentage commission costs more than a monthly plan once you pass roughly $20K a year in bookings.

| Layer | Recommended choice | Why | Watch-outs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Booking + deposits + waivers | A flat-fee charter tool (\~$50–$100/mo) with an embeddable widget, deposits, digital waivers and SMS reminders | Keeps margin and the customer relationship; embeds into the static GitHub Pages site | Verify iCal/Google Calendar sync and that deposits can be partial |
| Alternative | FareHarbor or Peek Pro | Polished checkout; Peek adds marketplace exposure | Commission-based, reportedly \~6% per booking, often passed to the customer; sales-led onboarding |
| Secondary channels | FishingBooker, Captain Experiences, partner-lodge front desks | Reach travelers already searching for White River guides | Commissions; you don't own the review history |
| Payments | Stripe or the booking tool's processor | Card + Apple/Google Pay | \~3% processing either way |
| Calendar | Google Calendar (two-way sync) | One source of truth across trips and TWT consulting work | Block travel time; the confluence is a long drive from central Arkansas |
| Conditions data | SWPA / Corps generation schedule for Bull Shoals; USGS Buffalo gauges | Drives go/no-go and the on-site conditions widget | Link to the official sources; never re-host stale numbers |

Policies to publish verbatim on the FAQ and in the checkout:

Policies to publish verbatim on the FAQ, at the review step, and in the waiver. They sit at the market norm: stricter than the first draft, more generous than Gaston's, and in line with the two marketplaces you may list on.

| Rule | Yours | Gaston's | Captain Experiences | Duranglers (guide shop) | FishingBooker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Deposit | 50% at booking | Prepaid, non-refundable | 15%, balance day-of | 50% | Vendor sets |
| Cancel 14+ days out | Full refund, minus card fees | No refund | Credit | Refund, minus fees | Vendor sets |
| Cancel 3–14 days out | Deposit becomes a 12-month credit | No refund | Credit (3+ days) | Deposit forfeited | Vendor sets |
| Cancel inside 72 h | Deposit forfeited | No refund | Deposit forfeited | Full trip forfeited (<48 h) | Vendor sets |
| No-show / day-of | Full trip charged | No refund | Full trip charged | Full trip forfeited | Counts as client cancel |
| Guide calls weather | Reschedule, a different tailwater, or full refund | — | Reschedule or credit (guide decides) | Rebook or refund (danger only) | Refund if unsafe |
| Late arrival | Trip ends on time | — | Late = no refund | — | Significant delay = no refund |

- **Deposit:** 50% at booking; balance the morning of the trip. Paying the deposit is acceptance of these terms and of the contact details on the booking.
- **Cancel 14+ days out:** full refund minus card processing (\~3%).
- **Cancel 3–14 days out:** deposit converts to a credit good for 12 months on any weekday.
- **Cancel inside 72 hours:** deposit forfeited.
- **No-show or day-of cancel:** full trip price charged to the card on file.
- **Guide cancels for conditions** (lightning, muddy water, heavy generation on all three dams): client chooses a reschedule, a different tailwater that morning, or a full refund. Your call, made by 7 PM the evening before.
- **Late arrival:** the trip ends at its scheduled time.
- **Licenses:** every angler 16+ brings an Arkansas license and trout permit.
- **Gratuity:** not included; 15–20% customary.

The "I never got your text" rule, so a missed message never costs you a day:

- Every confirmation, reminder and go/no-go goes by **both** SMS and email, from the booking tool, with delivery logs kept on the booking record.
- The booking form asks for mobile **and** email, and the waiver says: messages sent to the contact details on the booking count as delivered; keeping them current is the client's job.
- Reminders at **T-7** (ramp, map, what to wear) and **T-1 at 5 PM** ("Reply GO to confirm you're coming"). No reply by 8 PM → you call. No answer → the trip stays on and the no-show rule applies. Write that sentence on the site.
- A go/no-go is only ever a **cancellation or change from you**; silence means the trip runs as booked. Clients never have to receive a message for the trip to happen.
- Keep one comms log per booking (tool timestamps, or a `booking_events` table in Supabase) so a dispute is a record, not a memory.

Lodging: don't sell rooms. Sign two referral partners near the confluence (White Buffalo Resort is the obvious first call) and one near Cotter/Mountain Home, and list them on Plan Your Trip. Ask each to refer guests to you in return.

- **yamllint** plus a small schema check (e.g. `ajv` against a JSON Schema) on `packages.yaml`; reject a `live` package missing a `requires` credential.
- **Price-parity test:** build the site, scrape rendered prices, compare to YAML; fail on any mismatch.
- **Lighthouse CI** on `/guiding` with the ≥ 90 budgets from QA.
- Branch protection on `main`, one PR per change, GitHub Pages deploys only from a green `main`.

Useful agents once trips are running: a nightly conditions agent that pulls the generation schedule and Buffalo gauge into the site's conditions widget, and a post-trip agent that drafts the trip story from your photos and voice notes for you to edit and send.
